01. Context & Business Goal

Understanding Google Travel’s Ecosystem

Launched in 2019 by consolidating Google Flights, Google Hotels, and Google Trips, Google Travel generates tens of billions annually in referral ad revenue from airlines, hotel chains, and OTAs.

Business Context

Business Model: Targeted advertising and affiliate referral fees from airlines, hotels, and Online Travel Agencies (OTAs).

Google Travel's primary objective is to capture users during the "inspiration & decision" phase, serving as the default entry point for end-to-end travel research.

Target Audience & Goals

  • B2C Leisure & Business Travelers: Individuals planning complex vacations or managing spontaneous business itineraries.
  • Key Business Goal: Transition from a passive search engine into the primary, active operating system for trip planning and decision execution.

02. Problem Definition

The Cognitive Gap: Planning vs. Booking

Travelers are forced to bridge a disconnect between analytical planning tools and high-urgency transactional booking engines, leading to severe fragmentation.

1. The Planning Phase

Cognitive State: System 2 (Slow, analytical, calculating, budget-oriented).

Tools Used: Google Sheets, Excel, Notion, WhatsApp, Tripadvisor.

"Planning tools don't book. The data is dead — if a flight price changes or drops, my spreadsheet doesn't update."

2. The Booking Phase

Cognitive State: System 1 (Fast, urgent, transactional, emotional).

Tools Used: Booking.com, Airbnb, Kayak, Expedia.

"Booking tools don't plan. They push immediate checkout ('Only 1 room left!'), ignoring my overall budget and schedule options."

Core How Might We (HMW) Question

"How might we reduce cognitive load and fragmentation for users when moving from trip inspiration to booking execution?"

04. Foundational Research

In-Depth Qualitative Research (N=16)

We conducted 16 structured interviews with target behavioral segments to uncover friction points in pre-booking workflows.

Theme 01

The Discovery Gap

Users choose destinations based on thematic preferences (e.g., "beach vacation") rather than strict geographic endpoints.

"It's about the theme, not the place. If I want beaches, I'll compare alternative destinations that match the vibe."
— Andrew, Spontaneous Explorer (28)
Theme 02

Anchor vs. Whole

Travelers plan non-linearly. They lock in an "anchor" (e.g. flight) first, leaving lodgings open-ended.

"I don't book all hotels in advance. What if I like a place and decide to stay one extra day?"
— Andrew, Spontaneous Explorer (28)
Theme 03

Total Cost Blind Spot

Lack of automated scenario calculation forces heavy spreadsheet reliance for mental math.

"In spreadsheets, I calculate estimated total costs (Flight + Hotel + Car) to test out different itinerary schemas."
— Iztya, Analytical Planner (25)

Persona Synthesis

Iztya (The Planner)

Highly structured, budget-conscious group trip leader. Creates complex cost simulations in Google Sheets, sets price alerts across multiple date schemas, and desires shared collaborative dashboards.

Andrew (The Spontaneous Explorer)

Value-focused, solo/partner traveler. Compares destinations side-by-side (e.g., Miami vs. Puerto Rico), books "anchor" flights, and books lodgings dynamically on-the-go.

05. Strategy & Prioritization

Prioritizing Solutions via 2x2 Matrix

From an intense cross-functional ideation workshop (Crazy 8s & Problem Mapping), features were categorized by User Value vs. Technical Effort.

Quick Wins (High Value, Low/Med Effort)

  • Anchor Booking Logic: Allow flight bookings without nagging hotel prompts; switch UI state to "Open-Ended".
  • Active Trip Folders: Auto-group searched flights and hotels into editable pre-trip workspace dashboards.

Big Bets (High Value, High Effort)

  • Complex Scenario Planner: Multi-destination AI scenario builder comparing Option A vs. Option B with real-time dynamic total prices.

Fills-In (Low Value, Low Effort)

  • Trip Digest Notifications: Batch price drop updates instead of sending spammy individual Gmail alerts.

The Money Pit (Low Value, High Effort)

  • Tonight Mode: Panic-booking feature requiring real-time location-aware hotel availability.

07. Usability Testing

Validation & Metrics Plan

Moderated remote usability testing (N=5) utilizing think-aloud protocol to validate if the new experience eliminates the "spreadsheet reflex."

Test Tasks

  1. Task 1 (The Anchor Booking): "Book a flight to Miami to lock in price, but keep the trip 'Active' to add hotel later."
  2. Task 2 (Scenario Building): "Create two distinct trip options ('Luxury' vs. 'Budget') for a Tokyo group trip."
  3. Task 3 (Cost Comparison): "Compare total costs side-by-side without opening external calculators or notes."

Success Benchmarks

Metric Target Goal
Task Completion Rate > 90%
Time on Task < 2 mins (vs. 10+ mins in Excel)
Spreadsheet Reflex 0 users reaching for pen/calculator
Single Ease Question (SEQ) > 5.5 / 7 difficulty score
08. Takeaways & Impact

Key Learnings & Retrospective

1. Design for Mental Models

Users don't think in isolated silos (Flights vs. Hotels). Designing around the unified "Trip" concept matches natural user intent.

2. Reduce Mental Math

Applying Hick's Law by auto-calculating aggregated scenario packages empowers quick confidence without cognitive fatigue.

3. Support Non-Linearity

Accommodating "anchor bookings" recognizes that a trip isn't finished just because one component is locked in.
