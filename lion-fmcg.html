FMCG Market Assessment & Brand Revival Strategy: Lion Biscuits

Biscuito International led the Indonesian biscuit category in 2010 with Lion as its flagship product and Olio as a secondary line-up. Following a sales deterioration starting in 2015, a comprehensive diagnostic of 2016–2018 market data was conducted to re-establish Lion’s category dominance.

APAC FMCG vs. Indonesia Biscuit Growth

While overall FMCG nominal growth across APAC decelerated from 13.1% in 2016 down to 6.4% in 2018, Indonesia’s biscuit market demonstrated strong resilience with 9.4% value sales growth in 2018. However, value growth outpaced volume sales growth (2.3% volume growth in 2018), indicating that overall market value was increasingly driven by price adjustments or reduced promotional frequency rather than pure volume expansion.

Kantar Metrics Definition
Volume & value structure
Volume change
Change in the physical quantity sold between two periods, measured in weight or standard units rather than money.
Unit value change
Change in the average price paid per unit or kilo. It reflects shelf prices, promotion intensity and mix shift, such as shoppers trading up to premium biscuits.
Nominal growth
Raw growth in total value sales, before adjusting for inflation. It answers: how much more money passed through the tills than last year?
Sales performance growth
Volume sales growth
Growth rate of physical volume sold. Example: 1,000 kg to 1,050 kg is +5%.
Price growth
Change in the shelf price of the product or category. In theory it removes mix effects, unlike unit value change.
Value sales growth
Growth rate of total revenue. Example: $10,000 to $10,800 is +8%.

The decomposition rule: Value growth ≈ Volume growth + Price (or unit value) growth.

  • Healthy: +6% value from +4% volume and +2% price. Consumers are buying more.
  • Risky: +6% value from −3% volume and +9% price. Revenue grows only because prices rose, while the buyer footprint shrinks.

In this case study, APAC figures use unit value change and Indonesia figures use price growth as reported by Kantar, so the two price numbers aren’t directly comparable.

Lion maintained relatively high spontaneous brand awareness at 88%–93% but experienced progressive erosion in lower-funnel performance. Its 'top of mind' awareness dropped sharply from 52% in April 2016 to 33% in December 2018, while regular purchasing declined from 85% to 81%. More notably, its primary brand selection weakened, with the proportion of consumers who bought Lion most often falling from 39% in February 2017 to 27% in December 2018.

In contrast, Festo demonstrated the strongest conversion efficiency across every stage of the brand funnel. Consumer brand perceptions moved in direct alignment with overall sales performance, as Festo achieved significantly better funnel conversion than Lion or Olio. This suggests that improvements in brand awareness translated into stronger purchasing behavior, reinforcing Festo's gains in value sales and market share.

Festo: Sustained and Aggressive Campaign Rotation
Festo maintained a strong and continuous television advertising presence through multiple high-volume campaign bursts, including X6 (7,289 GRPs), X7 (8,030 GRPs), and X8 (1,359 GRPs). This sustained investment helped Festo continuously expand its TV Share of Voice (SOV) throughout 2016.

Olio: Steady Media Withdrawal
Olio gradually reduced its television advertising presence throughout 2016. Its campaigns included C5 (5,797 GRPs), C6 (2,968 GRPs), and another campaign execution with 2,906 GRPs. This declining investment was reflected in its Share of Voice, which fell consistently from 15% in Q1 to 11% in Q2, 9% in Q3, and just 3% in Q4.

Lion ("Be Passionate"): The Q3 2016 Media Blackout
Lion's television advertising activity was more intermittent, with 7,473 GRPs allocated to "Be Passionate" and another 2,906 GRPs to "Be Passionate II". Despite this investment, Lion went completely off-air in Q3 2016, recording 0% Share of Voice. Its SOV had already declined from 13% in Q1 to 9% in Q2 before dropping to zero in Q3, recovering only slightly to 8% in Q4. This quarter-long absence created an opportunity for competitors, particularly Festo, to capture greater share of voice during the period.

Key Diagnostic Findings
Media Share of Voice (SOV) Erosion

Competitor Festo outspent rivals on TV advertising General Rating Points (GRPs), expanding its Share of Voice (SOV) from 32% in Q1 2016 to 42% in Q4 2016. Over the same period, Lion’s SOV plummeted from 13% to 8%, while Olio dropped to 3%.

ImpactFesto paired heavy Above-The-Line (ATL) advertising with aggressive in-store activations, directly capturing market share from both Lion and Olio.

The Emotional Messaging Gap

Lion’s “Be Passionate” TV campaign focused strictly on child empowerment (“Gives my kid passion to do what he loves”). Meanwhile, competitors Festo and Olio deployed ads emphasizing family togetherness (“Inspires creating family moments” / “Everyone loves Festo”).

ImpactCampaigns featuring family togetherness and sharing moments generated higher purchase consideration than child-centered empowerment messaging.

Brand Imagery Priority Paradox

Lion retained top-tier performance in high-priority category drivers, leading in Brand Trust (82%), Favorite Brand (63%), and Great Taste (66%). However, Festo achieved market growth by dominating low-priority attributes where Lion was weak, such as “Is suitable for the whole family” (55% vs. Lion’s 47%) and “Enlighten the family moment” (52% vs. Lion’s 53%).

ImpactBy monopolizing family-sharing perceptions, Festo built broad household appeal despite lower baseline brand trust.

Pack Size Limitation & Declining Repetition

Lion experienced a steady decline in purchase repetition because its product portfolio was limited strictly to single-serving format packs (e.g., 17g, 20g, 21g, and 23g).

ImpactSingle-serving packs restricted Lion to individual consumption occasions, preventing the brand from capturing multi-person household sharing moments.

Strategic Action Plan (The Way Forward)

Channel & Activation Roadmap

  • E-Commerce Growth: Establish formal e-commerce partnerships to capitalize on FMCG online share, projected to grow from 7.3% in 2017 to 15% by 2025.
  • In-Store & Price Promotions: Deploy targeted in-store promotions to boost consumer purchasing power in response to value sales growing faster than volume.
  • Upper-Funnel Social Ads: Implement desktop and mobile display advertising on Facebook and Instagram to rebuild upper-funnel brand awareness.

Creative Narrative Refinement

  • Bridging Passion & Family: Evolve the “Be Passionate” narrative to explain why passion matters within the family context, improving emotional relevance.
  • Functional Benefit Layering: Complement emotional storytelling with explicit functional health claims, such as highlighting “Fortified with Vitamins and Minerals” alongside energy benefits.

Product Packaging Diversification

  • Multi-Serving Offerings: Introduce multi-serving family packs alongside single-serving options.
  • Household Sharing Position: Frame multi-person household consumption as a cognitive shortcut for purchasing the safest, most versatile family snack, directly driving purchase repetition.
A note on the data

This case study was developed based on a business case assessment provided by Kantar. To maintain data confidentiality and protect proprietary market insights, Kantar anonymized the brand identities (e.g., Lion, Festo, Olio) and masked specific market baseline figures. The diagnostic framework, quantitative analysis, and strategic recommendations presented here reflect my original work on the assessment.
