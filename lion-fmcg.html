FMCG Market Assessment & Brand Revival Strategy: Lion Biscuits

Biscuito International led the Indonesian biscuit category in 2010 with Lion as its flagship product and Olio as a secondary line-up. Following a sales deterioration starting in 2015, a comprehensive diagnostic of 2016–2018 market data was conducted to re-establish Lion’s category dominance

✦
APAC FMCG vs. Indonesia Biscuit Growth

While overall FMCG nominal growth across APAC decelerated from 13.1% in 2016 down to 6.4% in 2018, Indonesia’s biscuit market demonstrated strong resilience with 9.4% value sales growth in 2018. However, value growth outpaced volume sales growth (2.3% volume growth in 2018), indicating that overall market value was increasingly driven by price adjustments or reduced promotional frequency rather than pure volume expansion.

Key Diagnostic Findings
Media Share of Voice (SOV) Erosion

Competitor Festo outspent rivals on TV advertising General Rating Points (GRPs), expanding its Share of Voice (SOV) from 32% in Q1 2016 to 42% in Q4 2016. Over the same period, Lion’s SOV plummeted from 13% to 8%, while Olio dropped to 3%. Impact: Festo paired heavy Above-The-Line (ATL) advertising with aggressive in-store activations, directly capturing market share from both Lion and Olio

The Emotional Messaging Gap

Lion’s "Be Passionate" TV campaign focused strictly on child empowerment (“Gives my kid passion to do what he loves”). Meanwhile, competitors Festo and Olio deployed ads emphasizing family togetherness (“Inspires creating family moments” / “Everyone loves Festo”). Impact: Campaigns featuring family togetherness and sharing moments generated higher purchase consideration than child-centered empowerment messaging

Brand Imagery Priority Paradox

Lion retained top-tier performance in high-priority category drivers, leading in Brand Trust (82%), Favorite Brand (63%), and Great Taste (66%). However, Festo achieved market growth by dominating low-priority attributes where Lion was weak, such as "Is suitable for the whole family" (55% vs. Lion’s 47%) and "Enlighten the family moment" (52% vs. Lion’s 53%). Impact: By monopolizing family-sharing perceptions, Festo built broad household appeal despite lower baseline brand trust

Pack Size Limitation & Declining Repetition

Lion experienced a steady decline in purchase repetition because its product portfolio was limited strictly to single-serving format packs (e.g., 17g, 20g, 21g, and 23g) Impact: Single-serving packs restricted Lion to individual consumption occasions, preventing the brand from capturing multi-person household sharing moments

Strategic Action Plan (The Way Forward)

1. Channel & Activation Roadmap

  • E-Commerce Growth: Establish formal e-commerce partnerships to capitalize on FMCG online share, projected to grow from 7.3% in 2017 to 15% by 2025.
  • In-Store & Price Promotions: Deploy targeted in-store promotions to boost consumer purchasing power in response to value sales growing faster than volume.
  • Upper-Funnel Social Ads: Implement desktop and mobile display advertising on Facebook and Instagram to rebuild upper-funnel brand awareness.

2. Creative Narrative Refinement

  • Bridging Passion & Family: Evolve the "Be Passionate" narrative to explain why passion matters within the family context, improving emotional relevance.
  • Functional Benefit Layering: Complement emotional storytelling with explicit functional health claims, such as highlighting "Fortified with Vitamins and Minerals" alongside energy benefits.

3. Product Packaging Diversification

  • Multi-Serving Offerings: Introduce multi-serving family packs alongside single-serving options.
  • Household Sharing Position: Frame multi-person household consumption as a cognitive shortcut for purchasing the safest, most versatile family snack, directly driving purchase repetition.

Although I do not have data on purchase volume by brand, I can infer patterns based on advertising effectiveness, as outlined in the documents. Store activation emerged as a key driver of the market share gains Festo achieved at the expense of Lion and Olio. By combining strong store activation with ATL efforts, Festo successfully accelerated brand growth, delivering a notably greater impact than in the first quarter. Additionally, messaging that emphasized togetherness tended to increase consideration. This approach was adopted by Olio and Festo but was absent from Lion’s advertising, which may explain the difference in performance.
