Airbnb Persona Segmentation & Seasonal Content Strategy: Chicago

Chicago is an “underperforming favourite” on Airbnb: plenty of reviews, but listings sit open more often than average. This project clusters listings by amenities into seven guest personas and matches each one to a season, building a content plan to lift Chicago into the “high demand” quadrant.

Q1 Recap: What Drives Price

Seasonality shifts prices unevenly across cities. Chicago varies the most, while Austin is the only city to peak in spring, likely because of events like SXSW. An ANOVA showed room type and “accommodates” had the strongest influence on price, and adding “accommodates” improved prediction accuracy by 22%.

That pointed to two strategies: a location-based one with Chicago as the proof of concept, and a persona-based one built on variables that describe the stay itself.

How the Personas Were Built
Data preparation
Amenities with fewer than 100 occurrences were removed, the rest were grouped by hand from 514 to 40 categories, then merged to 27 where a category exceeded 100k occurrences. I resampled 5,000 listings per city (LA, NYC, Austin, Chicago, Denver, Dallas).
Demand proxy
Reviews in the last 12 months against availability over 365 days, split into four quadrants: high demand, underperforming favourite, emerging opportunity and low demand.
Why complete linkage on Jaccard distance
Amenity flags are binary, so Jaccard fits. Ward isn’t suited to dummy variables, and average linkage produced one dominant cluster. Complete linkage kept distant outliers from merging in.
Why eight clusters
I compared 8, 9 and 10. At eight, each cluster had a clearer z-score signature. One cluster (n=32) had no meaningful amenities, so I treated it as an outlier, leaving seven personas.

Key Diagnostic Findings
Price Variables Don’t Describe Preference

Room type and group size explain what a stay costs, not what the guest wants from it. Amenities were the best available stand-in for stay intent, so personas rest on what listings offer rather than on price tiers.

ImpactEach persona can carry its own message instead of a single Chicago pitch.

Chicago’s Demand Profile Is Split

Chicago combines high review volume with above-average availability. Backyard bliss and Family retreat sit closest to the high-demand quadrant, while Active leisure sits furthest out on availability.

ImpactBackyard bliss and Family retreat are the natural first targets for lifting Chicago’s position.

Each Persona Peaks in a Different Season

Active leisure’s median price reaches 305 USD in summer against 173.5 in winter. Backyard bliss rises from 115 in spring to 150 in summer, and Family retreat from 149.5 to 203.

ImpactA staggered persona-by-season calendar fits the data better than a single Chicago campaign.

The Personas Cover a Subset of the Market

A large generic group of listings (roughly 30k) has no distinctive amenity profile, and several personas sit close to the quadrant lines.

ImpactPersonas work as targeting angles for part of the market, and “high demand” labels need confidence intervals before they’re treated as firm.

Strategic Action Plan (The Way Forward)

Channel & Content Roadmap

  • Seasonal Blog Series: Highlight key trends, attractions and activities for each persona’s window.
  • Omni-channel Social: Run campaigns built on guest-generated content that shows each seasonal experience.

Positioning by Persona

  • Family Retreat: Outdoor fun and summer attractions.
  • Active Leisure: A deliberate off-season pitch, winter activity at the best price.
  • Workspace Serenity: The cheapest property type, built for a work-from-anywhere escape.

Measurement

  • Market Map Position: Track Chicago moving toward lower availability while holding review volume.
  • A/B Test: Compare persona creative against generic Chicago creative on booking conversion by season.

A note on the data

Demand here is a proxy. High availability can mean a blocked calendar, and review counts depend on how long a listing has existed. Equal samples per city make cities comparable but may distort each city’s real amenity mix. Next steps are adding listing age and calendar occupancy, testing persona stability across resamples, and using review text to check the persona names against what guests write.

Retrospective

The project turns a messy listing dataset into a business decision: a demand map, seven personas, and a calendar that says who to talk to and when. The strongest lesson was that a persona is only useful once it has a place, a season and a message attached. Next time I’d test cluster stability before building the plan on top of it.
